These are RACE TO MOUNT DOOM cards: sets 91 to 94, ninety-four of them. Each is a meta-site modifier ("Race Text 92_24") with a sentence or two of game text and nothing else: no cost, no stats but its type, no sheet row (the definition's own game text is all there is, and the packet says so). Every one has a test file already, written with the rig and not long ago.

WHAT KETURA ASKED FOR (2026-10-08, night): "These tests were done relatively recently, and so less effort should be required. I think that formatting and such may still be required, but these ... sets should overall require less attention." After the first batch (the old files had about three tests a card, and a missing decline test found F-0311 on three cards) she ruled on Q-0178, 2026-10-09: "I suppose it can't be helped if they weren't up to the standard. Let em get re-worked I suppose." So every check of the Standard that no existing test touches gets its test, and the existing tests are still kept and put in form, not rewritten. The work on a card is:
1. FORM. The existing file is brought to what lint and the Standard ask of any file: a bookend after the last play or answer of each test, the Choose/Accept/Decline/Pass methods in place of a raw string answer, the stats test and its header as the Standard has them, the naming and layout of the Standard, no exception relied on. `lint.py <id>` lists most of it.
2. THE LEDGER. Written as for any card, its claim lines pointing at the tests that exist.
3. GAPS, and only real ones. A clause or check of the Standard that NO existing test touches gets one test (most often: a negative the old file never asked, or the other side's scenario for a modifier that reads the same for both players). Where an existing test covers a check loosely but truly, it stands; do not rebuild its board or add a second test of the same thing for thoroughness.

WHAT NOT TO DO. Do not rewrite a working test in your own manner. Do not rename a test whose name already says what it shows. Do not drop an existing test unless it asserts something false (then it is a finding or a question, as your role file says) or repeats another test line for line; your report says what happened to each one (kept, kept and put in form, merged, dropped as a duplicate, contradicted). Do not widen a card's tests into the rules of the cards it touches: the helper's own behaviour is the helper's test.

THE PIPELINE IS THE USUAL ONE. Stage 1 is still planned from the text with the definition and the test file shut; with a sentence of text it is short, and it is what shows a gap the old file left. In stage 2 the existing file is your starting material: lay your plan beside it, tick off what it already has, and write only the difference.

HOW A MODIFIER COMES TO THE TABLE. `.claude/skills/gemp-testing/SKILL.md`, "Race to Mount Doom": the modifier is named to the scenario's constructor (for the Free Peoples player, the Shadow player, or one each) and the engine creates it in set-up; it is never cheated in, and it is `scn.GetFreepsCard("mod")` or `scn.GetShadowCard("mod")`. A modifier whose text reads the same for both players has a `GetFreepsScenario()` and a `GetShadowScenario()` and is tested from each; "Your ..." is shown for the owner and, once, not for the other player. A modifier that changes the bid or set-up takes the constructor's last argument (the decisions of set-up).

PARTICULAR TO THESE CARDS.
- Meta-sites cannot be hindered (Q-0110): no hindered board, no hinder test, and nothing in the ledger about it.
- A modifier has no culture, no cost and no uniqueness of its own: no test is owed for any of them.
- The stats test's header keeps the existing files' convention ("Set: RTMD 92", "Name: Race Text 92_24", "Type: MetaSite").
- Many modifiers change a RULE of the game (the bid, the hand size, what may be played from where, uniqueness). Where the text overrides a rule, the valid control is the same board without the modifier (most files have it already as "WithoutTheMod..."): keep those, they are the negative.
- Known: Race Text 92_29 ("Open and reveal a sealed booster pack ... You may play one shadow card revealed, ignoring all costs") is ruled to offer Shadow-phase events only (F-0193, with F-0186: a response event is never offered by "play an event from ..."); `lookup.py F-0193` before you plan it.
- A modifier that names a card title, a race or a culture is under the same rules as any card: race against culture (F-0019, an ad hoc culture on the invalid side), no two cards of one unique title in play at once unless the modifier's own text is what allows it (92_24, "Your Nazgul are non-unique", is such a text: there two copies of one Nazgûl are the test).
- No board outside play (the rulings index has it): a helper is given only what a printed card of its side could have.

A finding here is as anywhere: the text is the truth, a definition that does not do what it says is corrected at landing, a deliberate-looking departure or a real doubt goes to Ketura as a numbered question. Because these cards override rules, be slow to call the game wrong where the text is short and the rule it bends is long: `rules.py term ...` first.

HOW TO WORK: take the cards ONE AT A TIME. The blind packets of all your cards may be read together, as your role file says; from there finish a card completely (plan, stage 2, test file, ledger, lint, run) before the next card's stage 2 is opened, and after each card append one line to /home/claude/work/analysis/progress/<your batch folder>.txt (create it): the card id, its state, its test count, how many tests were kept, added and dropped, and each finding or question in a sentence. If your notes say a previous run on this batch was cut off, read that progress file, the plans it names and the files in the tree first, check what is there, and go on from it; do not start a finished card again.

TO THE REVIEWER AND THE LANDER. Judge the card by the Standard's checks, not by how much was written: a file that was put in form, given its ledger and one missing negative is a finished card. A gap you would fill is filled only if no test touches the check at all. Compare with `git show HEAD:<the test file>` to see that nothing the old file asserted was lost.
